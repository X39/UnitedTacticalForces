<objective>
Make the accrued session cost follow the provider's billing granularity (D-15, UAT gap 1), tracer-first: a provider-neutral billing unit declared by each runtime provider, Hetzner's started hour as the first implementation, applied by the accrued-cost calculator and proven end to end through the accrued-cost endpoint. Then pin the rounding rule at hour boundaries, across slices, after resolution and for a provider with another unit. Then prove that threshold crossings use the same value (a crossing at minute 1) and retune the UAT seed so the hour step is visible.

Purpose: CST-03 shows a wrong number today: Hetzner bills every started hour, so a session that ran 5 minutes has already cost a full hour, but the calculator prorates by elapsed time. CST-04 alerts depend on that number, so they fire late.

Output: `RuntimeBillingUnit`, `RuntimeProviderCapabilities.BillingUnit`, the Hetzner and emulated declarations, the reworked calculator and service, focused tests, and the retuned UAT seed.

Planner interpretation of D-15, recorded here and to be repeated in the summary: the user asked that the calculator "rounds each charged slice up to that unit". Rounding every 15-minute reservation slice separately to a full hour would bill four hours per hour, so the session's billable time, counted from its first charged slice, is rounded up as a whole to the provider's unit; the charged slice amounts price the covered part and the newest slice's hourly rate prices any billed time beyond it. Claude's discretion recorded here: the unit lives on `RuntimeProviderCapabilities` as an optional parameter (null means not declared), because capabilities are where a provider already declares pricing (`EstimatedHourlyCostEur`); a provider that declares nothing yields an unavailable amount (reason `billing-unit`) rather than a guessed one. The unit is resolved from the slices' ledger `ProviderIdentifier`, the durable record of who billed the time.
